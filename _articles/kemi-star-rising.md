Kemi Badenoch is getting more popular according to the polls, and certainly more popular than the waning party she leads, although a recovery is possible if Reform continues to falter.

The flirting with Andy seems to be over (was I the only one to notice that?) and she seems a bit more sure-footed and comfortable as leader.

Her takedown of Reform at the Tory party conference was impressive – **They are not like us, we are not like them**. It was quite powerful.

However she followed up later talking about how when John Barnes and Ian Wright played for England, their Englishness was not questioned.  
The savage racism that rained down from the terraces and bananas thrown at John Barnes was not questioning his Englishness, but whether or not he was indeed human. Bleak moments.

Attempts by politicians to appeal to the masses by referencing football never seem to quite land, for some reason.

Kemi is still finding her way and struggled when defining Tory defence strategy, rambling a little incoherently about a defensive dome which sounded very like being in Nato. Not a bad idea.

But Kemi is still intent on playing hardball and not buying into Andy Burnham’s attempt to make party politics more collaborative and productive.  
She asserts that cross-party collaboration is only possible when Labour propose something sensible, which emphatically means cutting benefits.

Until then Kemi is sticking with the status quo, arguing black is white, regardless of facts or what may be best for the country. She would disagree.

Remember Andy, Kemi is a Toughie, not a Toffee.
